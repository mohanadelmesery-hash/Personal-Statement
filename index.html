Unplugged

There is a certain type of emptiness that is created when you begin to feel as if you don't belong in a world that has already accepted you. I have struggled for many years to accept who I am as opposed to who I am always trying to become. I have faced rejection and discrimination on many occasions, and it eventually began to affect my life. During those times, I learned that I could only control my actions and what I learned from experience, but not the perception of me from others. The realization did not come right away, and it surely did not fix everything. However, it did provide the motivation I needed to do something that is meaningful to myself. I began to learn about music production, and shortly, it became more than just a hobby. I started writing everything I felt in notes, and day by day, those notes turned into songs. That was the first time I ever felt I accomplished something, especially when I saw my friends and family connect with the words and admire my talent. However, the true obsession began when I started getting more curious about the processes that allowed me to create music in the first place, which includes the conversion of electrical signals into sound waves and their manipulation. As my curiosity grew every day, I finally discovered that Electrical Engineering was the most relevant major to help me satisfy my creative need while learning new skills.

I want to master the physics behind each and every song and expand my knowledge to give every artist a voice exactly as it was meant to be heard.

When I initially started producing music, I had no clue how difficult it would be to record my musical ideas in a way that I would be satisfied with. Recording my vocals using just a simple microphone and free recording software such as BandLab became a boring task that I hated doing. Each recording attempt, I would continuously try to adjust and improve it in order to make it perfect in my ears, but in the end, none of them would turn out as I had hoped. At times, I thought that my ideas would never come to completion and that I just wasn't talented enough to record worthy material. There were many times when giving up and admitting defeat was the easiest escape from the discouraging results. However, over time, I learned how to shift my perspective on not only my recordings but my music as well. Instead of viewing my poor recording attempts as proof of my failures, I began to focus on what I did well and what could be improved for future tries. As a result, I gained a new perspective on my music and recordings, as well as an understanding of how to more effectively express my creative ideas. The most important lesson I took away from this experience is that doing it right isn't always the key to success. It is sometimes necessary to make mistakes in order to learn how to adapt or adjust your strategy for future efforts. This lesson has not only helped me record better music but has also helped me carry on with other tasks that I find challenging.

As I grew more experienced with music production, my interests began to shift from the art itself to the technology which produces it. How might compression affect the energy and character of a recording? Why did cutting certain frequencies make a vocal sound clearer? I began looking beyond the effects and instead towards the signals they were manipulating. I wondered how equalization could improve unclear vocals or how compression could change the range of sound levels, and I began to wonder how an electrical current could be shaped in such precise ways. With my studies in AP Physics and AP Calculus AB, I began to ask myself how these concepts could relate to waves and circuits, and subsequently developed an interest in the science of sound production. I have learned the importance of Electrical Engineering as a discipline which combines my two major passions for creative self-expression and critical problem solving in mathematics and physics. The field's attention to the creation and research of electronic circuits and its exploration of the technical details of audio production and mathematical modeling will help me not only further understand the processes which turn my voice into sound waves heard on the other end, but also design the technology which makes high-quality and accessible audio production possible.

What began with a pair of headphones, a cheap microphone, and a few sliders on a screen has transformed my understanding of both music and engineering. The desire to produce something I am proud of led to an interest in the instruments that support that production. I want to innovate by looking beyond present technological boundaries and exploring how new technologies and systems may be built to improve our understanding and better the world around us. My Electrical Engineering studies will allow me to keep studying the ways in which technology can be pushed to its limits. While I started learning music production to improve my own listening experience, it has inspired me to learn how to develop technology that allows voices to be heard.
